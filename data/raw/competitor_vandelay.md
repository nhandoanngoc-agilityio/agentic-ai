# Vandelay Logistics Intelligence — Competitor Profile

## Company Overview

Vandelay Logistics Intelligence sells vertical business intelligence for
freight forwarders, carriers and third-party logistics providers. Rather
than a general dashboard builder, it ships a data model of shipments,
lanes, carriers and invoices that customers populate from their
transport management systems. Vandelay reports about 300 customers,
almost all of them mid-sized logistics companies in North America and
Western Europe.

### Leadership and Headcount

Vandelay employs roughly 95 people. Its founders ran operations at a
freight brokerage before starting the company, and much of the staff has
a logistics background rather than a software one. Sales are handled by
a small team that attends industry trade shows and works through
transport-software resellers.

## Product and Pricing

Vandelay charges $70 per seat per month with a minimum of 20 seats per
contract. Contracts are annual and include onboarding, the prebuilt data
model and connectors to the major transport management systems. There
is no free trial; prospects are offered a guided demonstration on their
own sample data instead.

### Strengths

Prebuilt freight KPIs are the core strength: on-time delivery, lane
profitability, carrier scorecards and invoice accuracy are available on
the first day without any modelling work. Customers value that the
vocabulary matches their business, and the carrier-benchmarking views
are cited as something general BI tools cannot offer without heavy
custom work.

### Weaknesses

The market is narrow. Vandelay has little to offer a customer outside
logistics, and its general-purpose charting is limited compared with
horizontal BI tools. The per-seat price is high relative to
general-purpose products, and the seat minimum keeps very small
forwarders away. Integration with less common transport systems can take
months.

## Recent Moves

Vandelay has announced a carrier-data partnership that adds market rate
and capacity signals to its lane views, letting customers compare what
they pay with prevailing spot rates. It has also started offering a
managed-service option in which its own analysts maintain dashboards for
customers without in-house data staff.
