# Umbrella Analytics — Competitor Profile

## Company Overview

Umbrella Analytics builds embedded analytics for software vendors: SaaS
companies use its SDK to put charts, dashboards and self-service
exploration inside their own products under their own brand. Umbrella
reports about 650 customers, most of them vertical SaaS companies in
healthcare administration, property management and logistics software.
End users of those products rarely know Umbrella is underneath, which is
the point of the offering.

### Leadership and Headcount

Umbrella employs roughly 420 people, with a large solutions-engineering
group that helps customers design the analytics experience inside their
applications. Leadership has framed the company as infrastructure rather
than a BI tool, and it competes for budget with in-house engineering
teams as much as with other analytics vendors.

## Product and Pricing

Umbrella charges a platform fee of $2,000 per month plus $0.40 per
monthly active viewer, meaning each end user of the customer's product
who opens an embedded chart during the month. There is no per-seat
pricing for the customer's own staff, and the platform fee includes the
SDK, theming tools and a sandbox environment for development.

### Strengths

The white-label SDK is the most complete in this segment: customers can
restyle every component, control permissions from their own identity
system and ship analytics features without building a charting stack.
Solutions engineers are frequently praised for shortening the first
integration, and the multi-tenant data model fits SaaS products well.

### Weaknesses

Costs climb with viewer counts, so a customer whose product succeeds can
see its analytics bill grow faster than its own revenue. Several
customers describe renegotiating after a usage spike. The standalone
dashboard tool for internal reporting is basic, so most customers still
buy a separate BI product for their own teams.

## Recent Moves

Umbrella has introduced a usage-based tier aimed at smaller software
vendors that want to start without the full platform fee, and it is
piloting a natural-language query component that customers can embed.
Neither change has been rolled out to all customers yet, and the company
has not said when the new tier will be generally available.
